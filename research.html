Publications

  • Investment Opportunities, Market Feedback, and Voluntary Disclosure: Evidence from the Shale Oil Revolution

    with Jaewoo Kim and Bryce Schonberger
    The Accounting Review, 2026 101(2): 179-213

    Abstract summary

    The shale oil boom offers a setting to examine whether managers adjust disclosure to learn from investors. Oil and gas firms issued more capital expenditure forecasts and fewer earnings forecasts during the boom. Their investment decisions became more responsive to market reactions to investment forecasts, and those adjustments were more strongly associated with subsequent operating performance. The findings connect voluntary disclosure with managerial learning from stock prices.

    Source abstract

  • Redesigning Executive Incentives: The Rising Role of Subjective Performance Measures

    Dissertation
    AAA 2025 Competitive Manuscript Award
    The Accounting Review, 2026 101(1): 315–345

    Abstract summary

    This study examines the use of subjective measures in executive bonus contracts and their consequences. Machine learning identifies the types of goals these measures reward, including employee outcomes, culture, and executive performance. Following the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, firms increased their reliance on subjective incentives. The evidence links the growing use of these measures to greater CEO effort, stronger corporate culture, and innovation.

    Source abstract

  • Foreign Tax Holiday Participation and U.S. Job and Investment Loss

    with Linda Krull and Scott Rane
    Contemporary Accounting Research, 2026 43(1): 817-848

    Abstract summary

    This study investigates how participation in temporary foreign tax incentives relates to U.S. multinationals’ domestic activities. Participation is associated with more offshoring of U.S. jobs and lower domestic employment, capital spending, and research and development. These relationships are stronger among firms with less established foreign operations and differ from the effects of changes in statutory tax rates. The findings inform debates about international tax competition and resource allocation.

    Source abstract

  • Double Trouble? IRS’s Attention to Financial Accounting Restatements

    with Ryan Wilson
    Review of Accounting Studies, 2023 28: 2002–2038

    Abstract summary

    This study tests whether financial restatements attract IRS scrutiny. Using the acquisition of public filings to measure attention, it finds that the IRS is especially likely to obtain restatement announcements and does so quickly. Press releases and media coverage are also associated with increased attention. Greater IRS attention is linked to larger subsequent tax settlements and mentions of tax audits, suggesting that financial reporting problems provide useful signals for tax enforcement.

    Source abstract

  • Beyond Borders: Uncertainty in Supragovernmental Tax Enforcement and Corporate Investment

    with Martin Jacob, Jaron Wilde, and Ryan Wilson
    The Accounting Review, 2022 97(6): 233–261

    Abstract summary

    This study examines the investment consequences of uncertainty about supragovernmental tax enforcement. European Commission investigations into preferential tax rulings beginning in 2013 provide the setting. The evidence indicates declines in U.S. multinationals’ subsidiary investment in the countries under investigation, purchases from those countries, and aggregate U.S. investment flows to them. The results show that uncertainty about future enforcement can affect real business activity across national borders.

  • Discussion of ‘The Effect of Changes in Income Shifting on Affiliate Managers’ Internal Reporting Decisions’

    with Ryan Wilson
    Contemporary Accounting Research, 2023, 40(1): 120–165 (see Appendix 2)

    Abstract summary

    This discussion considers Klassen and Valle Ruiz’s study of how changes in multinational firms’ income-shifting strategies influence affiliate managers’ internal reporting. It places the study within research on the consequences of tax-motivated income shifting and focuses on the possibility that tax planning can distort incentives and reporting behavior inside the firm.

  • From Clicks to Bricks: The Effect of Economic Nexus Laws on Consumer Spending

    with John Robinson and Stephen Stewart
    3rd round, The Accounting Review

    Abstract summary

    This working paper studies how taxing online purchases changes shopping behavior. Comparing Washington’s adoption of economic nexus with neighboring Oregon, it finds an increase in Washington households’ purchases from physical retailers, particularly for taxable goods and near the state border. Vehicle-location data also indicate more visits to Oregon shopping centers. The results suggest that online sales taxation can shift spending toward physical stores and encourage cross-border shopping.

  • Tax Litigation as a Signal of Accounting Confidence

    with Jeremy Lee, Sean McGuire, and Ryan Wilson

    Abstract summary

    This working paper examines whether a firm’s willingness to litigate an IRS dispute signals confidence in its financial reporting. Using court filings, the study finds that litigating firms have lower incidences of fraud, restatements, and corporate violations. The signal is stronger in courts with broader discovery requirements. The findings suggest that management’s willingness to accept legal scrutiny can provide information about accounting quality beyond financial statements alone.

  • Tax Talk in Analyst Reports

    with Michael Drake, Travis Dyer, and Ryan Wilson

    Abstract summary

    This working paper examines tax analysis in analyst reports and its connection to forecasting skill. Tax discussion is driven largely by analyst and brokerage expertise rather than characteristics of the firms being covered. Reports with tax discussion have more accurate tax and earnings forecasts, and their forecast revisions generate stronger market responses without subsequent reversals. The evidence identifies specialized tax knowledge as a dimension of analyst skill that improves market information.
