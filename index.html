About

I am an assistant professor in the School of Accountancy at Brigham Young University's Marriott School of Business, where I hold the Andersen Foundation Fellowship. I specialize in corporate taxation and study how tax policy, tax planning, and tax enforcement shape business decisions.

My tax research spans international tax incentives, enforcement uncertainty, and the connections between financial reporting and IRS scrutiny. I examine how these issues influence where firms invest, locate jobs, and allocate resources. I also study how tax rules affect executive incentives and how firms use disclosure to learn from stock prices. My work has appeared in The Accounting Review, Review of Accounting Studies, and Contemporary Accounting Research.

I apply artificial intelligence, natural language processing, and advanced statistical methods to complex financial, business, and regulatory data. I welcome opportunities to bring this research perspective to collaborations with businesses and other organizations.

I earned my bachelor's degree at Utah Tech University, my master's degree at BYU, and my doctorate at the University of Oregon, all in accounting. At Oregon, I received the Doctoral Fellowship for Outstanding Doctoral Student. Before joining BYU in 2022, I was on the faculty at Texas A&M University's Mays School of Business.

My dissertation received the American Accounting Association's 2025 Competitive Manuscript Award.

AI & Data Analytics

My research increasingly applies artificial intelligence and advanced analytics to complex business and regulatory data. These methods make it possible to extract information and address questions that traditional datasets and approaches cannot easily capture.

Artificial Intelligence & Text Analytics

Applying large language models, natural language processing, and machine learning to corporate disclosures, regulatory documents, and other large collections of unstructured text.

Large-Scale Data Development

Building, integrating, and validating complex datasets to create new measures, identify patterns, and extract information from sources that are difficult to analyze.

Applied Statistical & Economic Analysis

Using econometric modeling, causal inference, and advanced quantitative methods to evaluate business decisions, regulatory changes, and economic outcomes.

Research applications

Examples from ongoing research and analytical method development.

Ongoing research

AI-driven analysis of corporate filings

I use SEC 10-K filings to study corporations' multinational operations. Large-scale text processing and language models help identify geographic references and classify business activities, turning extensive disclosures into structured data and new measures of corporate multinationality.

Ongoing research

Classification of regulatory documents

I use machine learning and language models to classify IRS private letter rulings and related documents. This work identifies tax topics and subtopics, assesses the relevance of regulatory developments, and explores their connections to publicly traded businesses.

Analytical methods developed

Data-driven decision support

I have developed analytical approaches that combine geographic, economic, and administrative data to examine humanitarian aid allocation across municipalities after natural disasters. This research uses predictive information and statistical modeling to study real-world resource allocation.
